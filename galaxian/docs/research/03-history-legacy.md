# Galaxian (Namco, 1979) — History, Context, Versions, Legacy

Research notes compiled 2026-10-05. Sources listed at the end. Items marked **(unverified)** came from search snippets only.

## 1. Development

**Release dates (sources disagree on Japan)**
- Japan: September 15, 1979 (Wikipedia, citing Namco's chronology). Arcade-History gives October 30, 1979; Giant Bomb/KLOV-derived listings say "October 1979". Safest phrasing: "autumn 1979".
- Europe: November 1979.
- North America: Namco demonstrated the game to Midway on October 17, 1979; Midway showed it at the AMOA show in November 1979; wide US release February 1980. The Midway operating manual is dated February 1980.

**Team (Wikipedia credits)**
- Designers: Kazunori Sawano (lead) and Shigeichi Ishimura
- Programmer: Koichi Tashiro
- Artist: Hiroshi Ono ("Mr. Dotman"), who improved the logo and bezel art before release and later did Galaga, Dig Dug, Mappy
- Sound: Toshio Kai. Namco's first arcade game with synthesizer-composed sound.

**Motivation**
- Namco president Masaya Nakamura ordered Sawano to produce the best "post-Invaders" game after Space Invaders (1978). Philosophy: "simple is best", gradual rather than spiky difficulty.
- Enemies were first modeled on Star Wars TIE Fighters, then redesigned to be insect-like.

**What was new**
- Marketed as the first video game with 100 percent true RGB color graphics. Disputed: a few earlier color arcade games exist. Safer: "one of the first full-color arcade games, and the first to make color central to the design."
- First arcade game on tile-based hardware (8x8 tiles) with hardware sprites, which made smooth sub-tile motion cheap.
- Multicolored animated sprites, animated explosions, a scrolling twinkling starfield, graphic icons for lives and stage flags.
- Enemies that leave formation and dive while firing. No shields. One player shot on screen at a time, which makes timing central.

## 2. Distribution and reception
- Midway held North American rights. Demand was so high Namco also licensed manufacturing to Taito and Irem in Japan.
- Cabinet types: upright, cocktail, cabaret/mini. This was the first use of the familiar "Namco cabinet" shape later reused for Pac-Man and Galaga.
- Sales: roughly 40,000 US units by 1981, 50,000 by 1982. Japan's #2 earner of 1979 and 1980. US #2 grossing arcade game of 1980 behind Asteroids.
- The Guardian (2021) ranked it the greatest video game of the 1970s.

## 3. Cabinet art and visual identity
- Upright: white cabinet, side art of the player ship about to shoot a green "dragonfly" flagship, painted in blue, green, and black (green matching the neon-green T-molding).
- Marquee: large blue and green "Galaxian" logo on glass, stylised neon lettering surrounded by planets and stars.
- The cabinet flagship is green/blue; the in-game aliens are blue (bottom 3 rows), purple, red, and a yellow flagship. Famicom and MSX ports made the bottom rows green.
- Logo description for a title screen (from visual familiarity, not citation-backed): "GALAXIAN" in heavy, flowing, slightly italic letters with rounded curves in a late-70s sci-fi style, tightly set and overlapping, filled with a green-to-cyan gradient and a light outline on a dark starfield.

## 4. Lore
- Attract text: "WE ARE THE GALAXIANS / MISSION: DESTROY ALIENS". That is the entire original story.
- Player ship: Galaxip. Enemies: Galaxians. Boss: Galaxian Flagship ("Galboss"). Red aliens act as flagship escorts.
- UGSF retcon (official ugsf-series.com): the United Galaxy Space Force timeline places Galaxian in AD 2279. Galaga follows in 2281.

## 5. Sequels, variants, and how Galaga differs
- Galaga (1981) differences that must NOT leak into a Galaxian recreation: enemies fly in at stage start (Galaxian starts in formation), two player shots on screen (Galaxian allows one), Boss Galaga tractor beam and dual fighter (none in Galaxian), Challenging Stages (none), enemies fire from formation in later Galaga stages (Galaxian enemies fire only while diving), hit-ratio stats (none).
- Gaplus / Galaga 3 (1984), Galaga '88 (1987), Galaxian 3: Project Dragoon (1990), Galaga Legions (2008), Galaga Wars (2016).
- Hacks and bootlegs: Galaxian Part X (Nichibutsu, faster fire, more random movement), Super Galaxians (faster), Moon Alien (Nichibutsu clone; Namco sued), Zero Time (Spain, very fast shots), T.T Spacian Part-2 (Taito).
- ROM set notes: older revisions allow aliens 2 shots in flight, later ones 3. Midway sets differ in bonus-life DIP tables.

## 6. Home ports
- Atari 5200 (1982, launch title): closest early port. Atari 8-bit shares the code.
- Atari 2600 (1983, General Computer Corp.): 8 enemies per row via a register-strobing trick; formation and dive-bombing preserved; reduced wave size.
- Atarisoft (1983-84): Apple II, C64, VIC-20, IBM PC, ColecoVision, ZX Spectrum.
- MSX, PC-8801, Sharp X1 (1984). Famicom (1984, Namcot's first Famicom title): all stages present, HUD shows 5 flags then switches to numerals.

## 7. Legacy
- The Galaxian Flagship became a Namco mascot: 6th bonus item in Pac-Man (2,000 pts), transform enemy in Galaga, bonus item in Dig Dug, Pac-Land, Pac-Mania, Pac-Man Championship Edition, Smash Bros.
- Film/TV: Big (1988), Joysticks (1983), Death Wish 4 (1987, sounds).
- Compilations: Namco Museum Vol. 1 and 3 (PS1), Namco Museum 64, DS, Remix, Megamix, Mini Player. Arcade Archives Galaxian (Hamster, 2022).
- World records: 1,114,550 (Gary Whelan, 2006); 1,653,270 (Aart van Vliet, 2009); 2,010,000 (David Lyne, 2016).

## 8. IP note
- Bandai Namco Entertainment owns Galaxian (code, art, sound, name, logo). US trademark GALAXIAN Reg. No. 2,095,564.
- Bandai Namco's 2015 "Catalog IP Open Project" was limited to Japanese companies. No general fan-game license exists.
- Practical implication: a non-commercial homage with original art, no ROM assets, and a clear "not affiliated with Bandai Namco" statement is the usual approach. It has no formal safe harbor.
